## Notes

AetherLove was built with Claude (Anthropic), and Github co-pilot as a pair-programming assistant
throughout development. Greptile was used as a pull-request agent to validate changes. This document is an honest account of where and how AI was involved.

**Overall level — `pair`:** The project author drove all product decisions,
reviewed every piece of generated code in full, and tested the plugin in-game
before each commit. AI acted on whole tasks but always under human direction and
with human sign-off.

### By area

**Plugin code (`pair`)** — UI screens, emoji/chat rendering pipeline, profile
system, onboarding flow, theming, settings, and the service layer were developed
collaboratively. The author read, understood, and validated all output.

**Translations (`pair`)** — English strings were written by the author.
The five non-English translations (Spanish, French, German, Russian, Portuguese)
were generated by AI and reviewed by a human native speaker before release.
Corrections from the community are still welcome via issues or merge requests.

**CI/CD configuration (`pair`)** — The GitLab pipeline (build, FTP deploy,
Dalamud repo-manifest update, GitHub public-mirror job) was designed with AI
assistance and manually validated by the author.

### What AI did not do

- Make product or UX decisions.
- Make architectureal decisions
- Write server-side backend logic independently (data model, moderation rules,
  and API contracts were designed by the author).
- Commit anything without the author reading and understanding it first.

### AI Use in assets
Three assets were generated the frontier OpenAI Image generation models:
- Aetherlove logo
- Aetherlove miniture logo
- "Allagan" phone bezel design. 

### Our stance

AI was a tool, not an author. The judgment, accountability, and creative
direction behind AetherLove belong to its human author. If you find a bug,
a mistranslation, or anything that looks wrong — open an issue. AI involvement
is never an excuse for something being incorrect and humans should always be in the driving seat, knowing exactly how things work. 

We also want to acknowledge something more uncomfortable: tools like these are
reshaping how software gets built, and that change is not uniformly good for
everyone. For some people in some roles, this represents a real shift in the
kind of work they have spent careers building. We do not think that is a small
thing, and we do not think it deserves to be brushed off with easy optimism
about "new opportunities."

What we can say is that AetherLove was built by a person who chose to stay in
the loop — who read the output, made the decisions, and takes responsibility for
the result. We think there is a meaningful difference between using AI as a tool
you understand and using it as a black box you just ship. We tried to do the
former. Whether the broader shift plays out well for everyone is a question none
of us can fully answer yet. We genuinely hope it does.
